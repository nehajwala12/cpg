1 · RECOMMENDATION

✔ PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS — at a first lien of $111–135MM (2.5–3.0x the corrected post-ClubCrest base; the top $24MM conditional on the rLTSA cliff landing at +30 months or later)

✘ DECLINE — at $150MM and above (the post-cliff test fails at every sweep speed) and at the working frame of $420–465MM (9.3–10.3x the corrected base; negative free cash flow in every year of the facility's life)

This is a decline on the attachment point, not on the asset.

The binding constraint is dated, not modelled: with the full one-time stack equity-funded ($80–107MM — separation + catch-up capex + ERP inventory build), years 1–3 clear to ~$204MM of debt — but the post-cliff year caps standing debt at ~$111MM when the rLTSA rolls off. A $120MM facility with a 100% sweep clears every period (~$12.9MM yrs 1–3, ~$14.7MM yr 4, ~$2MM post-cliff after sweep); nothing larger than $135MM does. At this attachment the loan is collateral-covered (1.3–2.9x asset cover, ~100% recovery), not cash-flow-covered — the mild downside ($7MM EBITDA) does not cover interest on any facility above ~$72MM, so #33 — carrying #73’s residual — prices the entire thesis, and a tangible-only asset-coverage test with springing appraisal rights joins the covenant package. Commercial reality, stated plainly: $111–135MM of debt against any plausible EV implies an equity cheque beyond the sponsor's $450MM fund without heavy co-invest (#86); Halcyon Ridge may well lose the deal at its price — on this arithmetic, that is the correct outcome. The ≥$150MM DECLINE is a current-record boundary — it becomes conditional (never automatic) only upon the #14-family waste terms, #29 validation, and #33 appraisals at ≥1.25x net tangible cover (§4.4a · §5).

At the frame it does not clear: cash interest alone consumes ~87% of corrected EBITDA before a dollar of capex; free cash flow is negative through the entire separation period and beyond; the facility does not de-lever; and a single ClubCrest reset — the most likely adverse event in the file — takes the credit to a leverage level that is no longer meaningful.

Four blocking gaps are absolute conditions: #14 · #29 · #33 · #86 (recomposed at Rev 2 — #73 → High, #33 elevated). If they are not answered, the recommendation is DECLINE at any attachment — not because the answers are presumed adverse, but because the Committee would be voting on an estimate. A flagged gap beats a guess. #69 (customer consumption ex-Marlowe's) stands at High — syndicated data is in hand and the seller's own materials disclose the channel unwind; the residual is ClubCrest POS and retailer inventory positions. #33’s appraisals carry #73’s residual — the Averlane topside, the listing-to-B/S tie, and the Nov-25 test file — alongside their own job.

Why — the recommendation reads this way

The band is sized to the weakest dated year, not the average year. The rLTSA cliff is contractual and dated: when it arrives, the post-cliff year supports roughly $111MM of standing debt — so the facility must be swept below that number before the calendar gets there. $120MM clears at every sweep speed; $135MM only if the cliff lands at +30 months or later; $150MM never does. No covenant can reschedule a contract expiry.

The loan is underwritten as collateral, because that is what the downside makes it. The mild case ($7MM EBITDA) does not cover cash interest above ~$72MM of debt — so the attachment must sit where recovery is arithmetic (~100% at 1.3–2.9x asset cover), which is why #33 — carrying #73’s residual — prices the entire thesis rather than the recovery tail.

Losing the deal at this price is an acceptable outcome. Winning at the frame requires adopting a seller bridge this diligence rejects line by line. A number that may not win, stated with its range, is exactly the information the Committee needs — a flagged gap beats a guess.
